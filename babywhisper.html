    Five cries worth learning by ear

    Hungry

    Short, rhythmic, low-pitched, and repetitive — builds gradually and pauses like it's waiting for a reply. Often paired with rooting or hand-to-mouth.

    Tired

    A whiny, nasal whimper that rises and falls unevenly, mixed with fussing, yawns, and eye-rubbing. Loses steam rather than building.

    Discomfort

    Fretful and intermittent — a wet diaper, an itchy tag, or too much noise and light. Stop-start, irritable, easing when the cause is fixed.

    Pain

    Sudden onset with no warm-up: a sharp, high-pitched shriek, then a breath-holding pause, then another shriek. Distinctly more intense than routine fussing.

    Gas / colic

    Tense, clenched-up crying that builds in intensity, often with legs pulled to the belly and a red face. Can be prolonged and hard to soothe.

    Attention / boredom

    Starts and stops almost on cue, softens the moment she's picked up, and often includes pauses to check who's listening.

    Read this before you trust the ring. Nightlull estimates a cry's pitch, loudness, and rhythm from your microphone and matches the pattern to categories described in infant-cry research — it is a listening aid, not a medical device, and it cannot diagnose pain, illness, or any condition. It will make mistakes. If crying is inconsolable, unusual for her, or paired with fever, vomiting, a rash, or lethargy, trust your judgment over this tool and call your pediatrician or emergency services. Audio is analyzed on this device only and is never uploaded or stored.
